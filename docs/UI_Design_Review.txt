GRID RESCUE — UI DESIGN REVIEW
October 18, 2026

DESIGN GOAL
Make a small rescue puzzle feel like a game, while making it easier to choose a
unit, read the board, understand the next action, and recover from mistakes.
Keep the accepted model and its rules as the source of truth.

BEFORE
The original real-view capture was inspected before the redesign. Its map used
wide rectangular cells filled with text, and almost every label was bold.
Terrain colors alone supplied most of the visual variety. Unit names and long
status text competed with the terrain and survivors. Pickup and delivery were
always enabled, so players had to discover their requirements through errors.
The board, roster, and details lacked a strong visual order. Starting over
immediately discarded progress, and a completed mission received only a small
status change. Passing model tests had not established a pleasant interface.

IMPLEMENTED CHANGES
- Give the map visual priority. Square tiles, recognizable terrain and unit art,
  survivor badges, and distinct legal-move indicators help a player scan it.
- Separate the three decisions: choose a squad member, choose a map destination,
  then inspect the selected unit and perform the available action.
- Use a restrained ivory and forest-green palette, consistent spacing, a
  bundled typeface, and short labels. Reserve visual emphasis for selection,
  available actions, the action budget, feedback, and mission outcomes.
- Make pickup and delivery available only when the selected unit can perform
  them; explain the next useful step. A waiting survivor is still collected
  explicitly, and delivery still requires the shelter.
- Let players select units through the squad, their map cells, or number keys.
  Keyboard map focus moves independently from the unit; activation is explicit.
- Keep the remaining action budget and rescue progress visible. Carried and
  rescued survivors are different states, and all rescues still require Command
  at the shelter before the mission can finish.
- Provide a readable Help screen in the game window, without losing progress.
- Confirm restarting a mission with progress, allow cancellation, and provide a clear
  way to play again after separate success and failure summaries.
- Preserve text labels, accessible control names, and visible keyboard focus.
  Color and artwork complement those cues; they do not replace the rules.
- Keep square tile proportions when the window changes size. Allow scrolling
  when the display is too small for the readable minimum layout. The Help
  content scrolls independently while its return button and keyboard guide
  remain visible.

EVIDENCE
The screenshots under docs/screenshots/ are produced from the actual Swing view
and actual button events, not design mockups. Milestone_3_Verification.txt records
the final build, automated behavior checks, visual captures, and runtime checks.
The 14-image capture set was visually reviewed at 1160 × 820, 980 × 760, and
1440 × 980. Checks included the full victory summary, updated unit names,
restart progress, readable compact controls, and scrollable Help content.
Milestone_3_Demo.txt gives a manual path through selection, rejection, pickup,
delivery, upgrades, a full victory, restart, help, and an optional default loss.
